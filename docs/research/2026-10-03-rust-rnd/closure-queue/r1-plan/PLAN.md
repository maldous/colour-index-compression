# R1 lane/stride closure

Prepared, unexecuted finite screen. R1 reuses the RSL v2 complete archive
contract with distinct `R1C1` magic: serialized operation program, nested CIX
archive or literal fallback, source/payload lengths and CRC over all preceding
bytes. Its decoder performs no search.

The frozen controls are generated by `fixtures.py` at exactly 8,192 bytes:
`strong` repeats adjacent values (lag-2 delta opportunity), `weak` mixes an
8-byte relation, and `absent` is fixed xorshift bytes. Their SHA-256 values
must be recorded before the one-shot encode/decode rows. These identities
never enter the encoder policy.

Each control evaluates 17 declared programs: identity, literal, each of lane
transpose/modular delta/XOR at lags 2/8/16, then six ordered depth-two pairs.
The pairs are transpose2/delta2 in both orders, transpose8/delta8 in both
orders, and transpose16/xor16 in both orders. This is a bounded screen, not
an exhaustive language search. Delta and residual are not duplicated.

Use one immutable CIXG1 `--fast --threads 1` backend for every native row;
literal stores raw bytes. Count the full R1C1 frame, nested CIX bytes,
instructions, CRC and literal bytes. The driver records candidate transform,
payload and complete sizes, child time/RSS, temporary files and exact fresh
`uncix` plus outer archive-only restoration. Reject malformed selected frames.

Root schedules one control at a time under one CPU, 1 GiB RAM, no swap, 64 MiB
temporary storage and 60 seconds per child. No averages or candidate repeats.
The selected result must beat identity in complete bytes on strong and restore
exactly; weak/absent losses are retained. This experiment cannot modify the
production selector.

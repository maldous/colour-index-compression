Last-chance compression methodology cross-check
2026-10-04 — literature and source inspection only

Finding

This review identifies useful distinctions and remaining unqualified avenues,
but no new measured CIX gain. It does not establish that compression opportunities
are exhausted. Broad family names such as LZ, context mixing or ANS are insufficient
to determine what the current implementation actually includes.

The Wikipedia overview [W] was read as a taxonomy and link index. Technical claims
below use original source, specifications or author papers. Its general-purpose,
media, executable and scientific categories are useful coverage prompts; dated
benchmark rankings and file-type descriptions are not release evidence.

UIQ: what the requested benchmark actually establishes

Mahoney's Generic Compression Benchmark [U] uses a secret-seeded collection of
short, delimited outputs of bounded random programs. Its displayed results are
historical (2008–2011). Scores divide compressed bytes by ppmonstr's output, not
input size. Its leader, uiq2_t4b followed by ZPAQ 1.04 with max_o2_msr settings,
scores 0.8750: 12.5% below that reference. The benchmark does not measure speed,
memory or decoder size, and entries may use different generated inputs. It is
neither a current general-purpose ranking nor a CIX result.

Actual source inspection [UT, UC], not inference from the route name:

* uiq2_t4b processes NUL-terminated bitstring records. It removes up to seven
  trailing zero bits, searches repeated suffix periods from 1 through 95 bits,
  omits a reconstructible one bit, and emits three-byte descriptors separately
  from remaining bits. Its decoder reconstructs the records and padding.
* max_o2_msr.cfg combines order-0/1/2 and sparse contexts, multiple mixers and
  secondary symbol estimation. Position modulo three enters its contexts.

Our interpretation: the three-byte descriptor layout makes the modulo-three
context natural. This is representation-conditioned prediction. It does not
demonstrate inversion of the random generator. The transform assumes a particular
record syntax and is not an arbitrary-file transform with a general fallback.
Its historical result cannot identify how much each individual component adds.

Random program outputs may have repetition and serialization regularities even
when program instructions are random. Recovering a short generating program is
a different problem from compressing its output statistically. Finite searches
over seeds, projections and functions do not exhaust program space. Conversely,
a finite generator benchmark cannot certify proximity to optimal compression.
The primary prediction paper linked from UIQ [L] concerns the limits of universal
prediction; it supplies no practical universal search algorithm for CIX.

Additional sources and their useful scope

1. Large Text Compression Benchmark [T], observed 2026-10-04, last-update label
   2026-09-30. It ranks enwik9 compressed data plus zipped decoder/assets; times
   come from heterogeneous machines. The listed leader is cmix-lex-transformer
   lexth11c at 95,836,613 bytes. Its description marks Hutter adjudication pending.
   This is a reported benchmark leader, not an independently reproduced result
   here, an awarded prize, a Silesia record or a CIX comparison.

   The author's README [LX] describes an int4 transformer combined with a
   cmix/PAQ-style model stack, quantization-aware retraining, pruning and CPU
   inference. It reports about 55.4 hours to encode and 59.4 hours to decode the
   billion-byte input. GPU training and GPU compression are separate: this
   compressor runs on CPU. Its archive includes its decoder and model assets;
   the separately counted compressor makes a different, 99,313,750-byte total.

   CIX implication: stronger learned predictors remain a real external frontier.
   The frozen CIX expert-mixture failures did not test or exclude this family.
   Target-corpus training with charged weights is not held-out generalization.
   Reuse would require declared training provenance, exact reproducibility,
   platform/resource qualification and complete costs. No new training or
   Hutter run follows from this observation.

2. lzbench [ZB] is an in-memory, common-build codec comparison that checks restored
   bytes. Its displayed Silesia tar is 211,947,520 bytes; local canonical member
   bytes sum to 211,938,580. Kanzi's own chart [K] uses a 211,957,760-byte tar.
   Those totals, file boundaries and reset policies differ. The pages are useful
   for throughput/Pareto and portability candidate discovery, not direct rankings
   against CIX's sum of complete per-member archives. In-memory codec timing also
   omits CIX's full CLI selection, framing and storage path.

3. Squeeze Chart [SQ] exposes mixed file-type and recompression comparisons, but
   its homepage's displayed updates are from 2018. Follow method/source links;
   do not promote its historical chart to a latest-record claim.

4. Precomp [PC] and preflate [PF] demonstrate a concrete route to further
   compression of some already-compressed assets: expose the underlying stream
   and retain reconstruction information sufficient to reproduce the original
   compressed bytes. Preflate predicts DEFLATE decisions and codes differences;
   it is a reconstruction library, not a general-purpose archive format.

   This is closely related to the user's residual/delta proposal, with an
   identifiable generating process. It is already partly covered by retained
   PAQ DEFLATE reconstruction. The project's fresh finite reconstruction closure
   found the two material gzip research wins were smaller with existing PAQ.
   Preserve that capability and its native integration requirement. JPEG/MP3
   and other reconstruction coverage must be checked separately; a DEFLATE win
   does not establish them. No new Precomp dependency is adopted by this review.

5. OpenZL [O, OP] is especially relevant to the required heterogeneous engine:
   typed intermediate streams, a graph of modular codecs, input-dependent
   selection, and a self-describing result interpreted by a common decoder.
   This is meaningful architectural prior art for CIX's paid graph/stream work.
   It is not the same as combining probabilities for one next symbol, and its
   tailored compression graphs are not proof of automatic discovery on arbitrary
   unknown files. Its repository explicitly distinguishes release compatibility
   from unguaranteed development commits. Earlier exclusion of an external
   transform interface was a release-scope disposition, not a negative benchmark.

6. ANS [A] and the Zstandard specification [ZS] distinguish probability modelling
   from coding efficiency and implementation cost. A different entropy coder can
   improve speed or finite-precision overhead without discovering a new signal.
   Zstandard already provides FSE internally. A native CIX rANS substream would
   be a separate unqualified implementation, not the first ANS-family capability
   in the portfolio. ANS's reverse coding order is also distinct from reversing
   source data to improve its statistical predictability.

The preliminary local audit incorrectly called DMC and ANS absent across all
backends. Direct PAQ source and libzstd call inspection corrected both statements
before this report. Only the narrower native/exposed-expert gaps remain. Similarly,
the old pending N3 label must not override its subsequent scoped closure.

What this changes about the opportunity assessment

There is no basis for claiming a universal compression limit has been reached.
There is also no basis for reopening every failed transform or mixing experiment.
The strong remaining engineering requirement is preserving and exposing useful
capabilities already present while finishing the integrated native product.
Distinct research possibilities include CTW/native expert exposure, richer learned
prediction, conditional models on paid representations, ROLZ, and unsupported
exact reconstruction formats. Their existence is not a measured marginal benefit
over the current complete portfolio.

The later RELEASE-PREDICTIVE-1 result remains a failure of its predeclared gate:
12.569558 ideal bits captured on 49,152 bytes (0.000256 bits/input byte). CPI1 also
retains its original frozen-pool closure. Neither excluded models nor transformed
domain predictions may be silently added to those oracles. HINDSIGHT ORACLE —
NON-CAUSAL DIAGNOSTIC ONLY remains the label for after-the-fact best predictions.

A future candidate needs a reason to predict residual errors or reduce a measured
cost, then a bounded comparison with the strongest available control. Charge
model/weights, reconstruction data, headers, boundaries and termination as applicable;
verify exact archive-only decoding. Do not turn published throughput on another
machine, ideal log loss, or a single reconstructed stream into a product claim.

Exactly one immediate action

Reconcile this corrected mechanism-level coverage with the existing RC50 research
review and native parity map, so release work preserves DMC/SSE/LZP/FSE and useful
reconstruction capabilities without falsely treating unmeasured families as closed.
The previously identified bounded nonlinear autoregressive residual hypothesis
remains a research proposal; this review neither executes nor promotes it.

No compression experiment, build, installation, corpus acquisition, benchmark
restart, production change, public publication or acceptance-ledger mutation was
performed. Only public documentation/small source files were read; ZIP source was
inspected in memory without executing its bundled program. Current benchmark HOLD
and the 55-item release register remain unchanged.

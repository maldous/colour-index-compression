Exact mathematical projections and recursive representation search
2026-10-04

Scope and conclusion

This extends the user's seed/offset/function hypothesis to roots, reciprocals,
other functions with arbitrary-precision binary outputs, and recursive search.
The objective is valid as a bounded search for a shorter lossless representation
than a particular existing compressor produces. It is not justified to conclude
that a random-looking archive is already globally minimal. A mathematical change
of coordinates can expose structure to a coder that previously missed it.

However, an infinite expansion is not itself a finite archive. A usable candidate
needs a finite description which reconstructs the original bytes exactly. New
patterns, a smaller numeric magnitude or lower marginal digit entropy alone do
not establish a smaller complete description. Infinite precision is not a source
of free information or a prerequisite for useful transformations.

No production code, real-data benchmark or closed numeric-composition branch was
changed. The small checks here use all integers of a tiny fixed domain to verify
precision and reconstruction, not to optimize CIX around synthetic results.

Two concrete meanings of an 'infinite precision representation'

1. An exact symbolic description: a rational, polynomial plus root selector,
   continued-fraction rule, expression tree, recurrence, or finite program which
   can generate as many digits as required. This is finite if all constants and
   rules have finite descriptions. Their complete costs must be counted.

2. A finite binary prefix plus a certified enclosing interval and an agreed
   inverse rule. Increase precision until exactly one original integer/byte
   string is consistent with the stored information, or transmit a residual/rank
   identifying the original among several candidates. Arithmetic can use exact
   integers/rationals or rigorous interval bounds. Ordinary floating-point
   agreement and a matching checksum are not proofs of uniqueness.

Symbolic and interval methods can be combined. Infinite real numbers generally
cannot be serialized literally. Arbitrary precision means that finite requested
precision may be increased; it does not mean finite memory stores infinitely many
digits. A function containing an arbitrary real constant has not compressed the
input if the input information is hidden in that constant.

Represent an archive as (length, unsigned integer), retaining byte/bit order and
leading-zero count through the length. X=0 and function-domain exceptions need
explicit handling. Sign, scale, normalization, branch selection, precision and
termination must be fixed by the format, derived from available information or
transmitted. Do not count the same already-required metadata twice.

Mathematical families beyond roots and reciprocals

Rational and fractional-linear maps:
  (aX+b)/(cX+d), other rational functions on certified injective domains.
  Fractional-linear maps with ad-bc nonzero and no pole on the domain have a
  rational inverse. Exact fractions may reveal common factors or simple rational
  relationships. Count numerator/denominator or coefficient descriptions; putting
  all of X in a denominator merely relocates its representation. Rational binary
  expansions are eventually periodic, but a long period is not a free short code.

Algebraic maps:
  rational powers, roots of low-degree polynomials, bounded radical expressions,
  and algebraic-number bases. Exact algebraic representation uses coefficients
  and an isolating interval or root selector. Small coefficients/relations can
  produce short descriptions. Writing 'root of z^2-X=0' stores X as a coefficient
  and is not a saving by itself. Different algebraic expressions for the same
  value should be canonicalized where practical.

Elementary transcendental maps:
  logarithm, exponential, log1p/expm1, arctangent and suitable monotonic branches
  of trigonometric/hyperbolic functions. They can generate infinite nonperiodic
  expansions and change distributions seen by a practical coder. Certified
  inverse intervals are required. Periodic or many-to-one real maps require a
  branch/domain or other disambiguation; do not assume that global real inversion
  is available. Restricting to a finite integer domain can change injectivity,
  but still needs a proof and an effective decoder.

Continued fractions and alternate bases:
  encode quotient sequences, finite/periodic rules or a certified convergent.
  These are alternate exact number representations, not new entropy coders.
  Some values have concise quotient/rule descriptions. Count quotient sizes,
  coefficients, termination and any precision needed to distinguish X. A finite
  continued fraction needs a canonical terminal convention. Base changes must be
  compared in actual bits, not numbers of decimal characters versus binary bits.

Series and iterative generators:
  power-series expansions, bounded recurrences, iterated maps and computable
  constants, including a second function applied to generated digits. A short
  initial condition plus rule can describe a long sequence. If the initial
  condition/coefficients contain X, account for them. Chaotic sensitivity may
  demand more parameter precision, and many-to-one iterations can require branch
  histories. Random-looking output is not itself evidence of useful prediction.

Exact discrete controls:
  invertible modular maps, binary linear maps and finite permutations provide
  comparison mechanisms without creating infinite expansions. Their role here is
  theoretical comparison and exact accounting. This does not reopen prior closed
  numeric structural-composition experiments or authorize new production routes.

For every family, admission depends on a finite decoder, proven reconstruction,
the complete byte cost and bounded computation. No assertion is made that every
member is useful, efficiently invertible or covered by one implementation.

Where root/reciprocal information moves

For an n-bit positive integer of typical magnitude 2^n and fixed k, the value
Y=X^(1/k) has about n/k integer bits. Near X, adjacent roots are separated on the
scale of (1/k) X^(1/k-1). Recovering the original arbitrary integer consequently
needs about n(1-1/k)+O(log k) fractional bits under ordinary fixed-point coding.
The total is about n+O(log k). This local-spacing argument does not exclude a
short symbolic expression or unusually compressible digit sequence.

For a reciprocal, adjacent inputs have separation
  1/X - 1/(X+1) = 1/(X(X+1)).
At n-bit magnitude this is about 2^-2n. A direct unnormalized fractional prefix
therefore needs about 2n places, with roughly n leading zero places. Those zeros
are easy to compress; their removal does not establish a saving over the original
n-bit representation. Exponent/scale coding is valid and must be counted fairly.

For logarithm at this scale, adjacent outputs differ by about 1/X, demanding
roughly n fractional bits even though the integer part becomes small. Saturating
functions can require much more precision. For example, a function approaching
a constant exponentially in X can push the distinguishing information extremely
far into its fractional tail. Smaller output magnitude is not smaller information.

For a fixed injective transform T on a finite source domain, probabilities are
relabeled: the Shannon entropy of the full transformed value is unchanged.
Marginal digit frequencies, numerical differential entropy or entropy per output
character can change. They are not interchangeable with the joint discrete
information needed to recover the original finite file.

Exact finite checks

The plan was saved before execution; all computations use exact integers.

Square root, all X=0..4095:
  Integer part alone has only 64 distinct values and loses information.
  q=floor(2^7 sqrt(X)) yields 4096 distinct prefixes, at most 13 bits long.
  Decode using the integer interval
    ceil(q^2/2^14) <= X <= ceil((q+1)^2/2^14)-1.
  Every interval contains exactly its original X.

Cube root, all X=0..4095:
  Integer part alone has 16 distinct values and loses information.
  q=floor(2^10 cuberoot(X)) yields 4096 distinct prefixes, at most 14 bits long.
  Inversion uses the same exact inequality with cubes and denominator 2^30.
  Every interval contains exactly its original X.

Reciprocal, all X=1..4095:
  q=floor(2^24/X) yields 4095 distinct prefixes. Decode by
    floor(2^24/(q+1))+1 <= X <= floor(2^24/q).
  Every interval contains exactly its original X. Zero is explicitly excluded.
  For X=2048..4095, the 24 fractional places contain 10 or 11 initial zero bits,
  while the 2048 distinct targets still carry 11 bits under a uniform distribution.

The root precisions are conservative sufficient choices, not optimal encodings.
The numbers above are prefix-integer widths or information counts, not physical
archive sizes. They omit fixed/shared transform/length/precision fields and any
actual digit compressor. They neither demonstrate nor rule out practical gains
from coding those prefixes with a more suitable model.

A genuine positive case

If X is a perfect square, its integer square root reconstructs it exactly with
no fractional tail. If an asset equals 3^1000, a format supporting integer powers
can describe it through the base, exponent and original byte length. Near-perfect
powers may be represented by a base/power plus a cheap signed residual. These are
real short-description opportunities when the full cost wins, regardless of how
random the ordinary binary digits may appear.

The toy perfect-square control includes every 12-bit input:
  64 perfect squares use a one-bit tag and six-bit root: 7 bits.
  4032 other values use a one-bit tag and twelve raw bits: 13 bits.
  Uniform average is 12.90625 bits versus 12 raw bits; every value decodes exactly.
This preserves the positive special case and its limited prevalence. It is not an
optimized code, a real-data prevalence estimate or proof of a CIX improvement.

Recursive search and what 'optimal' can mean

A chain is a finite reconstruction program. Charge its final payload and the
inverse instructions/parameters/residuals actually needed at every retained
stage. Do NOT charge full intermediate payloads if the decoder can regenerate
them; conversely, do not discard required earlier corrections or precision.

Some useful chains can temporarily expand. Rejecting every expanding intermediate
representation may miss a later net win. A bounded search can retain a limited
number of such intermediates and compare complete final descriptions, while
tracking time, memory, depth and certified precision. This remains an inference
about search design, not evidence of a useful chain on CIX archives.

Canonicalize obvious identities such as exp(log X)=X on the defined exact domain,
reciprocal of reciprocal, and compatible composed powers. Approximate implementations
cannot silently use exact symbolic identities unless their correction/rounding
semantics agree. Repeated states and equivalent programs should not consume
separate search effort indefinitely.

A chain whose complete stored bit length decreases at every accepted step has
only finitely many such steps. Finite-length archives do not permit an infinite
strictly descending sequence of nonnegative integer lengths. This does not mean
every intermediate transform must shrink or that the first local minimum is best.

For a fixed computable reversible transform with shared forward/inverse programs,
algorithmic description length is preserved up to constants: a short description
of T(X), followed by T^-1, already describes X. A practical compressor need not
find that description. Consequently it is possible to improve 'best for today's
coder', but impossible by definition to improve an actually shortest complete
description while retaining the same decoding conventions and side information.

A finite, total, resource-bounded grammar has a well-defined best admitted candidate
and can in principle be exhaustively searched, though cost may be prohibitive.
Unrestricted shortest-program search is not generally computable. We should say
'best found within the declared functions, parameters, precision and compute
budget', rather than claiming an absolute optimum or that all opportunities are
exhausted. Primary theoretical reference:
Vitanyi, How Incomputable Is Kolmogorov Complexity? (2020)
https://homepages.cwi.nl/~paulv/papers/incomp.pdf

Prior-art and implementation references

Wu et al. (2014), A Test Data Compression Scheme Based on Irrational Numbers Stored
Coding, describes storing circuit-test data through irrational-number expansions.
The indexed primary text discusses don't-care bits, so its results cannot be
transferred to arbitrary lossless files with every bit fixed. This is related
prior description, not verified evidence for recursive recompression of CIX.
Only indexed primary abstract/text passages were available during this review;
the full-text endpoint returned a browser challenge. No claimed ratio was adopted.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25258744/
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4166428/

Taurone et al. (2023), Lossless preprocessing of floating point data to enhance
compression, investigates conditions for invertible numeric preprocessing and
reports gains on its numeric datasets. It supports the distinction between a
useful source-specific representation and a claim about arbitrary compressed
bitstreams. Its results were not reproduced here or assigned to CIX.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03623

Johansson's Arb work provides arbitrary-precision ball arithmetic with rigorous
error bounds. Exact algebraic-number systems also use polynomial/root isolation.
These are ways to implement certified precision, not compression results. No
library was installed or selected as a distribution dependency in this work.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02831
https://flintlib.org/doc/qqbar.html

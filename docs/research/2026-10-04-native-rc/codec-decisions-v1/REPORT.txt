CODEC-DECISIONS-V1 — EXACT GZIP/DEFLATE DECISION RECODING SCREEN
2026-10-04

Question
--------
Can an existing gzip member be represented more compactly by preserving its
DEFLATE choices (block forms, dynamic-tree construction, literals and matches)
and separately compressing that decision representation, while reproducing the
original gzip bytes exactly?

Scope and fixed protocol
------------------------
The frozen plan is plan.json. The screen used only these existing local assets:

  artificial.tar.gz  76,723 B  cc1818a084d61fe693fe2a9db7312da25fdb4b54313502a6920abf692b7a47ab
  canterbury.tar.gz 739,071 B  f140e8a5b73d3f53198555a63bfb827889394a42f20825df33c810c3d5e3f8fb
  calgary.tar.gz   1,070,276 B e109eebdc19c5cee533c58bd6a49a4be3a77cc52f84ba234a089148a4f2093b7

The standalone Python-standard-library instrument records the gzip header and
trailer verbatim. It parses each DEFLATE block, including stored-block
alignment/LEN/NLEN/data, dynamic-tree header run decisions, literal and match
decisions, and final padding. Its inverse rebuilds the exact DEFLATE codes from
the stored tree decisions. CDV1 is the unsplit container; CDZ1/CDX1 wrap its
zlib-9/xz-1 form. GZB1/GXB1 are charged four-byte method-tagged ordinary
second-pass controls over the original gzip bytes.

The script first passed byte-exact stored, fixed-Huffman and dynamic-Huffman
synthetic members (types [0], [1], and [2], respectively). All three real
inputs then passed byte-identical gzip reconstruction and equal semantic
inflation for every decision candidate. Any physically smaller complete
candidate also passed a separate fresh Python process that received only its
candidate file and the expected source SHA-256.

---------------------

Best decision representation versus source:

  artificial: CDX1 is +18,369 B (+23.94%)
  canterbury: CDX1 is +98,849 B (+13.37%)
  calgary:    CDX1 is +470,648 B (+43.98%)

No decision representation is a physical compression win. This faithful
prototype exposes why: explicit per-token syntax expands a compact Huffman
bitstream, and generic second-pass coding does not recover that added cost.
It does not test a learned predictor that can recover decisions with a small
correction stream; it establishes that a direct syntax split alone is not an
opportunity on these assets.

The ordinary controls are real outer-wrapper results, not byte-identical gzip
members by themselves. Their inverse restores the original gzip bytes exactly:

  artificial GZB1: -425 B (-0.554%)
  canterbury GZB1: -106,442 B (-14.402%); GXB1: -110,671 B (-14.974%)
  calgary GZB1: -327 B (-0.031%)

These controls show residual redundancy in the selected historical gzip
archives. They do NOT validate the proposed decision-prediction mechanism,
and they cannot be attributed to CIX.

Resource record
---------------
Final frozen screen: 18.33 seconds wall, 18.25 seconds user CPU, 162,300 KiB
peak resident memory, one core (CPU 10), zero swaps. This stayed within the
60-second/600 MB/128 MB limits. Exact recorded values are in
benchmark-work/codec-decisions-v1/resources.txt.

Decision
--------
Close direct DEFLATE-decision recoding for these assets. The one evidence-led
follow-up, if this line is ever reopened, is a predeclared small causal
predictor-plus-correction representation for a bounded decision field; it must
beat both the complete gzip-byte second-pass control and the original bytes,
while retaining byte-exact inverse reconstruction. Do not build a production
route from this screen and do not claim novelty: DEFLATE-aware recompression
has established prior art.

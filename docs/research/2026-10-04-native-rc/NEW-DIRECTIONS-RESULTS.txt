New compression directions: completed bounded research screens
2026-10-04

Decision

Exact reconstruction of an existing gzip compressor's decisions produced a
real physical compression opportunity on two of three existing gzip assets.
The complete DCP2 research archives are 22.511% and 8.134% smaller than the
original Canterbury and Calgary gzip files and restore those original files
byte for byte. They also beat the two declared generic second-pass controls.
Artificial expands. This is established codec-aware recompression, not a new
information-theoretic principle or a qualified CIX result.

The requested center-out decoding, adaptive anchor schedule and bit-lane
partitioning did not pass their respective controls. Reversal delivered only
27 bytes of aggregate physical improvement on four exposed 4 KiB standard
source windows. Preserve these negative and limited results without promoting
them to a new CIX route.

Exactly one evidence-selected next action: qualify exact gzip/DEFLATE
recompression on a predeclared set of additional existing local encoder/settings
variants, against established DEFLATE-aware reconstruction controls, with a
deterministic reference specification and complete size/time/memory accounting.
That is a bounded follow-up research candidate, not an instruction to restart
the stopped RC benchmark or integrate this Python prototype into production.

Scope and evidence

The user explicitly requested trials of decoding-order and codec-decision ideas,
then requested reverse direction, every second/third/fourth bit, and center-out
decoding. The resulting five experiment directories accompany this report:

  decoding-order-v1                  causal ideal-loss and schedule replay
  directional-lanes-v1               physical reversible permutation frames
  codec-decisions-v1                 direct DEFLATE syntax recoding
  codec-decision-prediction-v1        reference prediction with raw corrections
  codec-decision-prediction-v2        completed entropy coding of that archive

These are small feasibility screens on existing, exposed local assets. They
are not pristine held-out qualification, a broad benchmark, or a leaderboard.
No production CIX behavior, third-party source or shipping files were changed.
Original data and binary research archives remain outside Git. Plans, scripts,
numeric results, resource records and negative outcomes are preserved here.

The v2 completion was motivated by a review of the v1 result: the raw marker
stream had not been entropy-coded. Its single xz-preset-1 completion rule was
declared before that run, but the overall research sequence was exploratory.
It must not be described as one fully blind, predeclared qualification trial.

Can middle-out work?

Yes, a decoder can start with a transmitted center value, decode its neighbours
and expand outward. Each new prediction may use values already recovered on
either side. Independent fronts need a defined dependency order; they cannot
use the still-unknown opposite frontier. An alternative is to transmit spaced
anchors first, then fill gaps from their known left and right endpoints. Anchor
information and any schedule/model selection must be charged.

Changing a fixed order does not create information: the chain-rule factorization
of the true joint probability is valid in any permutation and has the same
joint entropy. A finite practical predictor can nevertheless work better in one
order because useful context becomes available earlier. That is the plausible
opportunity tested here. Two directions are not automatically two independent
pieces of evidence about an unknown symbol.

The breakthrough algorithm in HBO's Silicon Valley is fictional, according to
Stanford's account of its scientific advisers:
https://stanfordmag.org/contents/silicon-valley-validity

A real project named middle-out implements segmented numeric time-series
compression using reference values and XOR differences, including SIMD code:
https://github.com/schizofreny/middle-out
Its name is not evidence that the fictional algorithm exists. That external
implementation was inspected through its documentation, not built or benchmarked
in this screen. General arbitrary-order lossless modeling also has prior art:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02037

Causal decoding-order result: ideal predictive bits only

One fixed neighbour-count model was used for forward, reverse, fixed-anchor,
adaptive-anchor and center-out schedules. Only already-decoded neighbours were
eligible; all anchor losses count. The adaptive order used exact integer
probability comparisons and never read a target before choosing its position.

Four standard 4,096-byte samples total 16,384 input bytes:

  Order                 Total ideal bits    Change versus best fixed direction
  Forward                     55,185.640
  Reverse                     54,576.666     strongest directional control
  Fixed anchors               55,777.758     +2.201%
  Adaptive anchors            55,542.250     +1.769%
  Center-out                  54,811.204     +0.430%

The three proposed schedules beat the strongest direction on zero of four
individual standard samples. All fail the declared gate of >=1% aggregate
improvement plus wins on at least three samples. Artificial cases are separate
instrument controls and did not select the method. Adaptive scheduling required
47,011-48,920 predictions per standard sample versus 4,096 for fixed orders.

Synthetic normalization, short lengths, and all 30 real sample/order replay
transports passed. Fresh processes reproduced the original bytes and the
position/probability trace hashes. The replay transport carries literal values;
it is NOT an entropy-coded archive. No ideal-bit result is reported as physical
byte savings. Independent read-only review found no material causal or
normalization defect. See decoding-order-v1/REPORT.txt for the full model.

Directional and bit-lane result: complete physical frame bytes

Ten transforms were paired with fixed zlib-9 and xz-1 coders on nine existing
4 KiB windows: four standard source samples, two artificial samples, and three
windows from previously compressed CIX assets. Transforms included byte/bit
reversal, center-out, and bit lanes of widths 2, 3 and 4, coded separately or
concatenated. Lengths, transform/coder IDs, stream framing and padding all count.

  Standard sample   Best identity frame   Best finished frame    Saving
  book1                         2,110                  2,107        3 bytes
  progc                         1,773                  1,773        0
  alice29.txt                   1,851                  1,851        0
  kennedy.xls                     731                    707       24 bytes
  Total                         6,465                  6,438       27 bytes

Both improvements came from byte reversal. The total finite-portfolio gain is
0.418% of the corresponding best identity frames, with selection already paid
in each frame. Choosing the smallest finished archive is a valid offline
portfolio, not an unpaid hindsight predictor. Center-out and every tested lane
variant failed to beat the strongest identity choice. Some lanes beat a weaker
same-coder control; those do not beat the strongest control. All three compressed
windows retained 4,122-byte identity frames with no transform improvement.

All 180 real candidate frames restored exactly, all lengths 0-17 passed, and
18 retained frames passed fresh-process inverses. These are research frames,
not CIX output sizes or evidence about entire source files.

Exact codec-decision reconstruction: complete physical archive bytes

The direct syntax approach in codec-decisions-v1 made every archive larger.
The subsequent predictor uses fixed raw-DEFLATE zlib 1.3, level 6, default
strategy and memLevel 8. It stores stronger xz-preset-1 compression of the
plaintext plus the information needed to recreate the ORIGINAL gzip member:
header, trailer, trees, block boundaries, padding and token corrections.

All literal/match decisions in these three inputs matched the reference at the
same plaintext offsets. Original block boundaries differed and remain paid.
This does not prove the reference will predict other encoders/settings equally
well. It is lawful to consult the recovered plaintext while rebuilding gzip:
the plaintext is fully represented in the archive and is decoded first.

  Asset        Original gzip   Best generic second pass   DCP2 complete
  Artificial          76,723                     76,298          80,508
  Canterbury         739,071                    628,400         572,700
  Calgary          1,070,276                  1,069,949         983,216

  Asset        DCP2 change vs original     Change vs best generic control
  Artificial        +3,785 B (+4.933%)                         +4,210 B
  Canterbury      -166,371 B (-22.511%)                       -55,700 B
  Calgary          -87,060 B (-8.134%)                        -86,733 B

Generic controls are zlib-9 and xz-preset-1 on the original gzip bytes, with
their four-byte wrapper IDs counted. They are not the strongest established
DEFLATE-aware reconstruction tools. DCP2 is exactly four bytes "DCP2" followed
by xz-preset-1 compression of the whole DCP1 archive. Every stored component is
paid, including the inner compressed plaintext, reference-version field and
all corrections. Shared decoder/program size is not added to these conventional
archive-byte comparisons; this is not a self-extracting program or Prize score.

Fresh decoder processes read only DCP2 plus an expected hash used for checking,
restore the contained plaintext, regenerate the reference decisions, apply
stored reconstruction information and emit byte-identical original gzip.
All three original gzip SHA-256 hashes match. Source/output hashes are retained
in the experiment evidence. Independent read-only review found no remaining
material accounting or decoder-dependency defect.

Two implementation findings are preserved. DCP1 initially left one raw byte per
correct token prediction; its larger sizes could not reject compact correction
coding. DCP2 supplies that missing entropy coding. Separately, v1's correction
encoder failed to advance the plaintext offset across a stored DEFLATE block;
its decoder already advanced correctly. The three real files contain only
dynamic blocks and their measured streams were unaffected. V2 fixes the encoder
and passes a new nonempty stored-block followed by fixed-block reconstruction
test. Old executed code/results remain unchanged; the report records the fix.

The gain comes from a stronger representation of the underlying contents while
retaining exact original compressor decisions. It does not compress arbitrary
random strings, demonstrate the proposed infinite-precision transform theory,
or establish a new entropy coder. Exact reconstruction with predicted decisions
and corrections is established in preflate-style tools:
https://github.com/microsoft/preflate-rs

The prototype depends on this Python/zlib runtime. Rejecting a different zlib
version is a useful guard, but a version string alone is not a durable portable
format specification. Malformed-input hardening, encoder diversity, native
integration, separate encode/decode throughput and stronger specialized controls
remain unqualified. No CIX production gain, world record or novel theory follows.
